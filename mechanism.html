mechanism

Sarrus mechanism

Geometry, actuation, measured response, build state, and motion examples for topology-programmable soft robotic surfaces and bodies.
Mechanism Monolithically printed pneumatic Sarrus-linkage-inspired cell with compliant links, flexural joints, and integrated PneuNet actuation.
Assembly Four cells form a 2 x 2 module with cross-cell pneumatic channels and snap-fit module connectivity.
Function Local pressure-driven expansion and module bias produce programmable shape, stiffness, surface waves, object manipulation, crawling, and rolling.

mechanical path

Geometry, constraints, and load path

  • The unit cell uses a compliant Sarrus-linkage-inspired geometry so pressure-driven expansion rotates linkage legs while the soft pneumatic actuator drives deformation.
  • The cap reduces top-surface gaps between adjacent cells; module-scale leg orientation creates a repeatable bending bias.
  • The public mechanical characterization includes pressure-dependent expansion, force-displacement response, hysteresis, and local stiffness for cells and module arrays.

build

Build and actuation

  • The physical module is a printed pneumatic prototype, not a speculative rendering.
  • The actuation assumption used in the public paper is tethered pneumatic input; integrated electronically addressed actuation remains future work.
  • The current public record gives figure-level measurements here, with fuller numeric detail in the manuscript and supplementary figures.

motion

Motion examples

  • The repeated cell/module architecture produces more than a static soft-robotics sample: it creates repeatable physical behaviors.
  • A surface or body can visibly change shape, move an object, generate a travelling wave, or produce body-scale motion from the same module architecture.
  • The clearest object-manipulation example is pickup and drop: the body lowers around an object, transports it through a curved posture, and releases it elsewhere.
